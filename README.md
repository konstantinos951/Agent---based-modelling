# Agent---based-modelling
MATLAB projects from my MSc Financial Engineering at the University of Glasgow, exploring agent-based models, wealth exchange, limit-order books and zero-intelligence traders, with explanations, simulations and analysis.


This repository brings together my MATLAB projects developed while studying Agent-Based Modelling in Finance as part of the MSc in Financial Engineering at the University of Glasgow. It documents my progress in understanding how individual decisions, interactions and market rules can generate collective economic and financial outcomes.

The projects explore agent-based models through simulations. Rather than assuming that every participant behaves optimally, these models describe agents with specific rules and examine the patterns that emerge when they interact. My focus is on understanding the underlying mechanisms, implementing them clearly and interpreting the resulting behaviour.

The initial projects cover three areas. A wealth-exchange model examines how a distribution of wealth evolves when agents randomly transfer money while total wealth remains constant. A minimal limit-order book demonstrates how incoming buy and sell orders interact, how outstanding quotes are stored and how transaction prices are determined. A zero-intelligence constrained trader model introduces buyer valuations and seller costs to investigate how random quotes, combined with auction rules and restrictions against loss-making trades, affect realised surplus and allocative efficiency.

Throughout the repository, I aim to keep the code accessible and the modelling assumptions explicit. Each project is intended to include an explanation of its purpose, the main variables, the simulation process and the interpretation of its outputs. As the portfolio develops, I plan to add experiments comparing parameter choices and random seeds, alongside visualisations and discussion of model limitations.

These projects connect my undergraduate background in Mathematics and Statistics with my developing interests in quantitative finance, financial markets and computational modelling. They provide experience in translating mathematical ideas into simulations, tracking model outcomes and distinguishing economic interpretation from numerical output.

This is a learning portfolio based on university coursework, with scope for independent extensions. AI tools support parts of the coding and learning process, while my emphasis remains on understanding, reviewing and explaining the implementations. The models are simplified educational representations of markets and economic interactions; they are not trading strategies or investment recommendations.

The repository will evolve as I complete further coursework, improve existing implementations and develop more detailed investigations into financial market behaviour.
